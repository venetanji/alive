# Failed current-boot pilot: bounded regression triage

The current pilot remains inadmissible: collector projection 2811.566 s exceeds the 2700 s cap and the 5% headroom requirement; 41 callback processing-budget exceedances are actually observed. The old pilot also failed the headroom requirement at 2606.020 s. This analysis does not authorize a run or change either result. The counters are processing-time measurements, not physical underruns; the evidence does not establish their cause or dismiss them as platform noise.

## Narrow next action

Review and, if accepted for a separate implementation task, consolidate the two overlapping process-group collectors into a single owned collector with a timestamped, synchronized publication of its actual snapshots. `desktop_sustained.py:102` already samples the app tree, sampler/capture tree and private audio server on a one-second schedule; `check_alive_desktop.py:664` independently creates the same three `ProcessSampler`s, and its endurance dwell calls the latter on the driving thread at `check_alive_desktop.py:1205`. Both enumerate `/proc`, inspect process status, FDs and `smaps_rollup`, and check identities (`stream_measurement.py:59`). This is concrete duplicated collection, not a claim that the existing accessible-reference cache is missing.

Preserve the current resource fields, process and identity caps, cadence, timestamps, CPU coverage/gap reporting, health and failure propagation, and record provenance. Retain legacy output consumers, including `process_tree_samples` and `resource-samples.jsonl`, by recording actual fresh published observations with their original timestamps and coverage; never relabel a stale sample as a fresh read. Account explicitly for the different startup, boundary and terminal snapshots rather than silently dropping them. Keep the separate editor-resources caller working. Preserve every original action/assertion, the 180 s dwell, transport/epoch observations, credit gaps, recording and all hard caps and forecast policy. A design that cannot preserve these obligations is not a valid correction. Add focused deterministic tests of publication, freshness, error propagation, caps and output compatibility before any separately authorized live pilot.

This is a bounded overhead correction candidate, not an established explanation of the failure or a promise of admission. In the current cycle window, the legacy collector completed 458 snapshots, with 45.380 s summed across its three sequential `sample_duration` fields (18.988 s app-tree portion); the background collector completed 607 snapshots with 61.735 s summed sampling duration. The former blocks the driver; the latter is concurrent, so these durations overlap and cannot be summed as CPU time or credited as a forecast saving. The old cycle similarly had 456 legacy samples and 42.161 s summed group sampling duration. Actual reduction, callback effects and forecast remain unmeasured. No further pilot follows from this report.

## Comparison and accounting

The same reported binary, module, wrapper, driver and staged example hashes were used. Collector fixed cost, computed as collector wall minus cycle checkpoint interval, rose from 337.303 to 398.421 s (+61.117 s); cycle wall rose from 568.629 to 607.213 s (+38.583 s), while cycle credit rose from 494.842 to 497.389 s. Fixed cost is setup plus post-cycle work, not purely setup. The forecast rose by 205.545 s; its growth is consistent with both higher fixed cost and greater cycle wall per credited second, amplified by the existing linear scale and 10% variability. These differences alone do not isolate a cause.

`journey-timing.json` reports inclusive, overlapping query totals, so they must not be added: walk 187.797 -> 243.168 s, scoped find 126.623 -> 171.325 s, wait-for 123.012 -> 167.711 s. Their maxima increased too; they are wall timings and do not identify CPU consumption or a missing cache. The current resource sampler recorded a 9.734 s maximum scheduling delay and 6.122 s maximum duration across the whole journey, with coverage gaps; transient work can be missed. Cycle-window background sampling's maximum duration was 0.413 s and missed-cadence value zero, so whole-journey maxima should not be attributed to the cycle.

Cycle-window playing JSONL had old/current 901/880 positive credits and 23/52 zero-credit records, with 5/6 recorded intervals over the 2.5 s credit limit. The independently audited positive intervals have zero invalid intervals and zero gap/action crossings in both runs. Cycle-window gaps include 43/56 action boundaries and 72/95 invalid transport reads (each also has a corresponding shape record). They are conservative exclusions, not proof of stopped audio or lost credited time; do not bypass them.

The 500 s target produced four versus five complete cycles, including a short fractional tail: after cycle 3 the recorded played values were 499.358 versus 498.693 s. The existing loop checks credit before completing each cycle and performs its ordinary boundary actions even on short tails (`check_alive_desktop.py:1184`); its action count and cycle cost are consequently sensitive to a small residual. Scaling this short-tail fixed action cost linearly is conservative and may overstate an 1800 s journey, but neither deleting the tail nor changing pacing, extrapolation or target is justified by these receipts. The fifth cycle is not by itself a demonstrated harness defect.
