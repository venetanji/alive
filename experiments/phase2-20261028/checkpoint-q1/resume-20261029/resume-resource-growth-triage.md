The persistent Alive parent accounts for the persistent FD increase; the two persistent WebKit processes end at their initial FD counts. The samples record FD counts only, not FD targets/types, so no particular handle owner or leak is identifiable. The last-only group was observed from 858 to 903 seconds; earlier similar groups appeared and disappeared. Other transient WebKit workers include PID 1094651 (23 samples, 145–167 seconds, roughly 256–264 MiB RSS and 66 FDs) and several lasting 1–4 samples. Peaks and aggregate fluctuations thus also depend on the observed cohort. The collector retains discovered identities across reparenting, but can miss short-lived or not-yet-discovered processes; `stream_measurement.py:59` reads `/proc` sequentially and uses PID/starttime to guard reuse. Twelve app-group samples report enumeration gaps or unavailable processes; these are not necessarily missing app identities. The previous report also records sampling delays and missed cadences. RSS sums count shared mappings repeatedly; PSS is apportioned, and its shifts can reflect other processes sharing mappings.

The first and last samples span 903.029 seconds, including startup, walkthrough, endurance and terminal work; they are not repeated equivalent workload states. For example at 452 seconds (168.8 proven playing seconds), the persistent Alive and web processes had 367896 and 413564 KiB RSS, respectively; at 678 seconds (363.8 proven seconds), they had 368708 and 428892 KiB. That observed interval shows +812 and +15328 KiB, but includes different actions and process states and gives no repeatability or causal slope. Alive then falls to 301960 KiB at the last sample while the web process reaches 435228 KiB. Best-effort action/playing labels are unsynchronized with resource reads. One run and these snapshots cannot establish a leak, bounded steady state, or per-cycle growth rate.

The separately sampled observer/recorder group must not be added to app growth. Its persistent Python observer 1088801/7648110 grows 46168 to 76080 KiB RSS, 26237 to 50917 KiB PSS, and 9 to 15 FDs. Recorder supervisor 1097205 and `pw-cat` 1097208 are present for approximately 572 seconds and gone at the last sample; other short-lived Python children appear. The private PipeWire server 1088104/7647188 remains the same identity: 7148 to 8652 KiB RSS, 1641 to 2656 KiB PSS, and 26 to 26 FDs; it rises to roughly 15676 KiB RSS and 40 FDs during recording. These are separate process groups, sampled at different instants, and do not identify causal ownership of app changes.

The bounded source inspection finds candidates for allocation churn, not a demonstrated retention defect. `apps/ultra/ui/alive.mjs:608` schedules the next poll only after the queued refresh completes; refresh obtains a snapshot and renders (`:127`), and disposal stops future scheduling (`:619`). `apps/ultra/ui/alive-editor.mjs:75` validates and renders each hosted snapshot, with serialized 100 ms polling (`:79`) and disposal support (`:82`); render replaces cable/path nodes and reuses or disposes node views (`:44`). `apps/ultra/src-tauri/src/session/desktop.rs:455` opens/focuses a generation/instance-labelled editor; its Linux destroy handler disconnects the documented manager/view signal cycle (`:474`), and generation changes close stale windows (`:492`). The editor binding map retains an entry on close until generation reset, with an existing label overwritten when reopened; these samples do not establish accumulating distinct bindings or quantify their memory/FD impact. No actionable concrete leak is evidenced.

If further diagnosis is necessary, after the current lease completes run a separate controlled repeated same-state measurement with fixed workload and matched warm checkpoints: compare per PID/starttime PSS/RSS and per-process FD target/type inventories before and after repeated identical hosted-editor open/close cycles, plus an equal-duration no-editor control, recording window lifecycle and transient cohort coverage. Repeat independently, include post-close settling, and distinguish retention from cache/allocator growth. This is a proposal for a later bounded diagnostic, not a change to the active qualification or its caps. No running process was inspected and no tests, builds, GUI or audio were started in this review.
